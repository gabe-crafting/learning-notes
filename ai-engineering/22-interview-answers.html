Interview answers

Answers written out the way they should be said, not the way a textbook would write them. One section per question: the spoken answer, the follow-ups it invites, and the number to finish on.

How to use this page Rehearse each one out loud until it sounds like talking, not reciting — an answer that is obviously memorised reads worse than a rough one. Keep them short: sixty seconds is plenty, and a crisp answer invites a better follow-up question than a rambling one. Every answer should end on something you actually built, with a number in it. How do embeddings work?

Say this — about 40 seconds

An embedding is a list of numbers that represents the meaning of a piece of text. You send text to an embedding model and it returns a fixed-length vector — a few hundred to a few thousand numbers. It is trained so that texts with similar meaning come out pointing in similar directions.

That is the whole trick: once meaning is a direction in space, comparing meaning becomes geometry. You measure the angle between two vectors — cosine similarity — and that number tells you how related they are.

It is why semantic search beats keywords. Someone searching "how do I cancel" will not match a page titled "terminating your subscription" — zero words in common — but their vectors land close together.

In practice I embed every chunk once at ingestion and store the vectors in pgvector, then embed the incoming question and ask the database for the nearest ones.

The follow-ups it invites

If they askSay
How does the model learn that? It is trained on pairs of texts that should be related, and pushed to put those close together and unrelated ones far apart. I use embedding models, I do not train them.
So why use keyword search at all? Embeddings are bad at exact tokens — product codes, error numbers, rare names. That is why I run hybrid: vector plus BM25, fused with RRF.
How did you pick the embedding model? I measured recall on my own labelled set rather than trusting a leaderboard. More dimensions is a cost and storage tradeoff, not automatically better.
What are the failure modes? Negation is weak — "contract includes indemnity" and "excludes indemnity" embed almost identically. And the same model has to be used for indexing and querying, or the vectors are not comparable; changing it means re-indexing everything.
What does the similarity score mean? It ranks, it does not grade. 0.82 is not "82% relevant" — the distribution differs per model and per corpus, so any cutoff has to be calibrated on your own data.

Land on a number

Finish with something measured rather than something read: "on my project, recall@5 was 0.61 with vectors alone and 0.84 once I added BM25." That turns a definition into experience, and usually ends the questioning there.

Do not oversell the maths Claiming to know how the embedding model is trained internally invites exactly the question you cannot answer. "I use them, I do not train them" is a strong answer, not a weak one — it is the honest line between AI engineering and ML engineering, and the interviewer is usually checking that you know where it sits.
